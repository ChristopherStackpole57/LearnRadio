01. Introduction

Welcome to the World of Radios

Radio is one of the most revolutionary inventions in human history.

Using electromagnetic waves in the radiowave (3Hz-3THz), radio allows users to send and receive information wirelessly and over massive distances; in fact, amateur (ham) radios can reach distances of over 300 miles when using powerful equipment. Predating the first smartphones by nearly a century, radio transformed the way humanas connect and communicate over vast distances.

Radio devices come in many different shapes and sizes, ranging from small hand held radios (walkie-talkies/handhelds) through base stations with dedicated towers. Due to their revolutionary nature, radios have seen extensive use in both civilian and military applications. Operating as long distance communication networks for civilians, radio allowed the rapid transportation of information over growing industrial powers. Likewise, at the turn of the twentieth century radio was adopted by various military institutions seeking a low-cost, long-range, and rapid way to communicate status updates, orders, and mission-critical information.

Radio is, however, not without its problems. By nature, radio utilizies a one-to-many relationship (one user emits, many receive). Consequently, any messages sent to your intended receipients can also be received by unintended receipients as well. This fact, being detrimental and even dangerous in military applications, spurred the development of modernc ryptography. Radio cryptography, which first appeared during the first world war, comes in many forms. While the history of cryptographic methods is beyond the scope of this page, the impact of radio on the development of modern cryptology is undoubtedly massive.
